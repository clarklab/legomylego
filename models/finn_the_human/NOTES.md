# Finn the Human

Finn from Adventure Time as a lanky, posable brick-built figure: 121 pieces, 19.4 cm tall
(11 cm across from sword to waving hand). Our own design, worked out from three pictures: a
white hat with two round ears, a face built sideways from ordinary tiles, noodle arms and legs
one stud thick, a round two-tone backpack and his golden sword. His hips, shoulders, elbows,
forearms and head all turn.

## Source

- **From:** three pictures and a brief from the owner: "a properly lanky Finny, in under 200
  pieces, bonus points for posable arms and legs". No plans.
- **Designer / credit:** ours. The character is Pendleton Ward's (Adventure Time, Cartoon
  Network). It copies no one's model: LEGO's own Finn (set 21308, LEGO Ideas Adventure Time)
  is squat like a BrickHeadz and its face is a printed piece no longer sold, and this is
  neither.
- **Saved in `reference/`:** nothing, on purpose. The three pictures stay in
  `inbox/finn-the-human/`, which is local only: `preview.png` (the cartoon, running: the
  proportions and the face), `preview-2.jpeg` (a 3D render, standing with his sword: the pose,
  the boots, how wide the backpack is), `official.jpg` (LEGO's set: its colours only).
- **Taken in:** 2026-10-08 by finn-agent.

## What had to be worked out

Everything: only the look was given.

- **Proportions (sure).** Head and ears 29% of his height, shirt and shorts 25%, neck 3%,
  legs and shoes 43%. The head is 6 studs wide, the body 4 wide and 2 deep, arms and legs one
  round brick thick. Six leg bricks were tried in the numbers and seven kept: he is meant to
  be lanky.
- **Skin: Light Nougat (sure).** It is the cartoon's colour; Tan (LEGO's choice) reads as
  khaki. Every skin part exists in it: the round brick (in 5 sets, the one we need most of:
  22), the 1L bar with clip (9 sets), the 1 x 2 plate with a clip on its side (11) and the
  round plate with a handle (3, the thinnest). None is flagged rare. No ball joint, click hinge
  or 1 x 1 clip plate was ever made in Light Nougat, which is why the elbow is a clip on a
  handle and why no joint part shows in skin. If the round bricks prove hard to buy, Tan has
  all of these except the 1L bar with clip (1 set).
- **The face (sure).** No printed part. A 4 x 4 plate stands on its edge on a brick with four
  side studs, 4 LDU back from the hat's rim, so the hat wraps the face. Eyes: round 1 x 1
  black tiles on 1 x 1 plates at the two ends of the middle row (a stud closer together was
  rendered first: it looked like a smiley, not Finn). Mouth: tile 1748 (1 x 2 half circle),
  flat edge up, in Dark Red (4 sets, the latest in 2026; Black and Red are the common
  fallbacks).
  Eyes and mouth stand one plate proud of the face, so no white shows at their corners.
  Tried and dropped: the eyes half a stud lower (on jumpers on end). They touch the corners
  of the mouth and the grin is lost.
- **The ears (fairly sure).** 2 x 2 domes, standing a plate deep in the top of the hat. The
  first version (a round 1 x 1 plate and tile) was too small for a head this size. A 1.5-stud
  dome (20952) is the size in between but trips a false technique warning in the engine (its
  stud hole is described from the top), so it was not used.
- **Shoulders and hips: Technic pins (a decision).** The first version had Mixel ball joints
  at the shoulders and click hinges at the hips. The ball and socket plates were only ever
  made in grey and read as armour pads; the click hinges' fingers stick out 4 LDU past their
  plate's studs, so nothing flush can cover them. Now each sleeve and each shorts leg is a
  1 x 1 Technic brick in the shirt's or the shorts' own colour, on a black friction pin whose
  ends are inside. Nothing grey or black shows from the front; from the side you see the end
  of each pin in its hole. What was given up: arms that lift out sideways (they swing forward
  and back only), and the click of a locking hip (see below).
- **The backpack (sure).** 8 studs wide so it shows behind both arms from the front, as in
  the pictures; the 6-stud disc tried first hid behind them. Lime half circle above, green
  below with round corners, a curved slope on each for the bulge. Its mount is a wedge plate
  whose cut corners clear the tops of the legs when they swing.
- **Legs that do not fold (a decision).** Eight round bricks on one stud each would bend, so
  two 4L bars run down through their open studs into the hollow stud of the shoe.
- **The sword (stretch goal, done).** A 1 x 8 tile for the blade; the grip is the bar of a
  round plate, which is bar-thick for only 14 LDU: the hand's clip and the jewel's stud just
  fit. He holds it out to his right so that it never crosses his face.

### The joints, and how far each turns

Measured by building other poses (no overlap, still one piece):

| Joint | Made of | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Hips (each) | 2780 pin between two 6541 Technic bricks | forward 50 degrees, back 15 (the backpack) |
| Shoulders (each) | 2780 pin between a 3700 and a 6541 | forward 100 degrees with a straight arm, back 15 (the backpack) |
| Elbows | 3484 clip on the handle of a 26047 | 0 to 170 degrees (the forearm sits a stud to one side of the upper arm) |
| Forearm turn | the 26047's stud in the upper arm | all the way round; out to the side or forward are clear |
| Head | 3680 + 3679 turntable | all the way round |
| Sword | 11476 clip on the 32828's bar | turns in the hand |

## What I am unsure of

- **Friction pins under a heavy head.** The head is 42 g of his 92 g. Standing straight, the
  weight above his hips sits 2 mm in front of the hip pins, well inside what a friction pin
  holds, but a click hinge would hold a walking pose harder. It was the price of hiding the
  joint.
- **Stability: 6.6 degrees backwards** (8.4 forwards, 8.6 and 10.1 sideways), against the
  default 10. `min_tilt_deg` is 6 in model.toml, with the numbers. Reaching 10 all round would
  take shoes 6 studs by 3. He stands; he will not survive a nudge to the head.
- **The pin ends** show from the side: a black dot on each sleeve and each hip.
- **Only the right hand is a hand** (the plate with the clip that holds the sword). The left
  arm ends in its last round brick.
- **Dark Red 1748** is in 4 sets only. Black (111 sets) is the swap if it cannot be had.

## Parts and price

121 pieces in 59 lines; every one has a LEGO element ID and none is rare
(`real_elements`: 59 part/colour combinations, 0 not real, 0 rare). The rough estimate from
`brickkit all` is $6 to $21 new on BrickLink before shipping. Most of one colour: 22 round
bricks in Light Nougat.
