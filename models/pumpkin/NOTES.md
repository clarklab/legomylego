# Pumpkin

A Quick Bricks model: a jack-o'-lantern in 51 pieces (22 lines), 5.4 cm wide, 5.3 cm deep and
6.8 cm tall with its leaf (4.6 cm to the stem plate). It is a cube of plates with a rounded look:
square black eyes, a mouth with three black teeth, an orange rind of layered plates on every
side, and a green stem with a leaf. It is built sideways: a grey core with studs on all four
faces, and panels of plates that go on those studs from the side. The design is BrixBuild's
(Rebrickable MOC-163756); this is our own 3D drawing of it, made from their instructions.

`design.py` has the sub-assemblies of the plans (mouth panel, head half, eyes panel, side
panel, stem). All 8 checks pass, and `brickkit status pumpkin --check` says the build is `clean`.

## What had to be worked out

Sureness: **high** means the pictures show it and the finished model matches them; **medium**
means a deduction; **guess** means the pictures do not say.

- **Steps 1 to 12 are drawn upside down (high).** The pictures show the undersides: the tubes
  of the tiles, bricks and plates face up (steps 1, 2, 3, 4), and the bricks show the square
  holes of their undersides. The base half is built upside down on its tiles (studs point down,
  into the tiles' undersides) and stays that way: its bottom is smooth. The head half (steps 9
  to 12) is drawn upside down too but is the right way up in the finished model (step 13 turns it
  over, and its top shows studs). So the halves meet underside to underside, with the 1 x 4 plates and
  the 2 x 2 plate of the head half hanging below its grey 4 x 4. I checked by rendering the
  model at step 18 and putting it next to page 9: the hollow head half, the 16-high slot between
  the halves, the orange strip hooking down at the front edge and the flat grey end of the base
  half all match.
- **Step 2: where the bricks go (high).** The notch shapes in the picture (open at the front and
  back, closed at the ends) fix it: the four 1 x 1 bricks are in the corners and the 2 x 4 brick
  lies across the middle two rows. The head half (step 10) has only the four corner bricks, so
  all four of its faces are hollow in the middle.
- **Step 3: the plates (high).** The plate layer covers the whole core except the two middle
  cells of the front edge.
- **Steps 5 to 8: the mouth panel (medium).** The teeth plate's studs go into the corner plates'
  undersides (the plates' own studs show in step 6, so they are in front of it), so the teeth plate
  is the layer nearest the core, hidden behind the bottom row. It sits back in the front
  hollow of the base half; only the corner plates' end columns meet the side studs of the
  corner bricks. The teeth stick up into the notch. They stop 1 short of the underside
  of the orange 1 x 4 strip above (it is at height 40, the teeth reach 39).
- **Heights of the panels (high).** A side stud is 10 below the top of its 1 x 1 brick (the brick
  is flipped in the base half), so it lands in the middle of a row of a panel. That fixes the
  mouth panel at the bottom (rows at heights 8 to 48), the eyes panel above it (48 to 88) and
  the side panels across the whole height (8 to 88, four rows). The four-row side panel's four
  corner studs reach both halves' studs.
- **Step 12: the 1 x 4 plates (high).** On page 6 they sit on the left front edge of the
  upside-down head half; on pages 7 and 9 they are the orange strip that hangs under the front
  of the finished head half. Grey is under the grey 4 x 4 plate, orange under the grey.
- **Step 13: the green plate (high).** The picture shows the 1 x 1 stud at the back cell of the
  2 x 2 (looking from the front right); step 31 shows it at the left from the front left. It is
  the back left cell.
- **Steps 14 to 16: the eyes (high).** The jumper plates' studs are in the middle of two studs,
  so the eye tiles sit half a stud off the column grid.
- **Step 18: nothing holds the halves together (high).** Where the halves meet both have their
  undersides, with no stud to push into a hole. The pictures show the same, and the 16-high gap on
  the right face. They are held only by the side panels (step 22 and 29), which have studs
  into both halves. In `design.py` steps 18 and 22 are therefore one step, "the head sits on the
  base, and a side panel across the back ties them", because the build check needs every step to
  leave one piece. The head half has `insert=(0, -1, 0)` (set down from above) and the two
  hanging pieces under its grey 4 x 4 have `insert=(0, 1, 0)` (from below) so the order inside
  a step comes out right.
- **Steps 22 and 29: which face gets which panel (medium).** Page 12 is drawn from the back
  (the raised green plate is nearest, and the eye tile shows at the far edge), so the first
  side panel goes on the back face. Page 17 shows the second on the right face and the edge of
  the third on the far left. Since the three panels are identical it does not matter which is
  which.
- **Steps 23 to 28 (high).** The panels look the same as the first, drawn from other angles
  (page 15's even looks mirrored, which changes nothing: the panel is symmetrical).
- **Step 30: the stem (medium).** The leaf clips on the horizontal bar of the plate's handle
  (they connect: 1 clip connection in the checks). A clip lets the leaf swing about the bar, and
  the pictures show it standing up and curving away at some angle, so the angle is a guess:
  15 degrees off upright, leaning forward over the face. The handle points to the front
  (toward the face), as on page 19.
- **Colours (high).** The parts page names them: Orange, Light Bluish Gray, Black, Green (the
  dark green) and Bright Green (the leaf only). They are all real part and colour pairs
  (the `real_elements` check passes; none is rare).
- **The leaf's name (high).** The parts page says 16770; the catalogue calls it a creature body
  part, "Barb Large (Claw, Talon) with Clip". It is the same part.
- **Part 3023.** LDraw draws it as `3023b`; it is the same plate 1 x 2.

## Quick Bricks

- `[quick]` in `model.toml`: the oak desktop in the window room, in evening light (the settings
  asked for). 14 seconds, clicks only. With 51 pieces it is over the 25-piece limit, so the parts
  fly in instead of starting laid out on the table.
- `highlight = ["stem", "eyes"]`: the stem with its leaf (it goes on last) and the two eye
  tiles.
- `brickkit status pumpkin --check` says the build is `clean`. Worth a look in the preview:
  the head half is one sub-assembly that lands on the base half with nothing to click into (it
  only joins up when the back side panel goes on in the same step), and its pieces go on in the
  plans' order, the top plate first and what hangs under it after.
- `collection = "quick_bricks"` is left commented out until the owner says to publish.
