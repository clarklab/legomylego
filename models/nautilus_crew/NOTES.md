# Nautilus Crew: design notes

Captain Nemo, Ned Land, Professor Pierre Aronnax and Conseil, the main characters of Jules
Verne's *20,000 Leagues Under the Sea*, as LEGO minifigures dressed loosely after Walt
Disney's 1954 film, with one of Nemo's crew in a brass deep-sea diving helmet (the film's
undersea scenes), on a slice of the Nautilus's deck in front of one of its salon windows. A
companion to the [Nautilus](../nautilus/NOTES.md) model: the same reddish brown iron, dark
brown walkway, Pearl Gold trim and the same salon window (a gold round 4 x 4 frame, clear
glass, a ring of eight yellow lights). The characters come from the public-domain novel; the
costumes follow the film loosely and no actor likeness is claimed.

- **106 LEGO pieces** in 51 part/colour lines, **5 minifigures** among them (each a head, a
  torso assembly, a legs assembly, headwear and something to hold: 25 pieces).
- **12.8 x 6.4 cm** base (16 x 8 studs), **9.3 cm** to the top of the bulkhead; the figures
  are the usual 4.4 cm. About 140 g.
- **Price**: roughly $29-$120 for new parts on BrickLink (price bands, `out/price_estimate.md`;
  the diver's three single-set parts are priced up as scarce, and are usually sold used).
- Checks (`out/report.html`): connections, collisions, buildability, stability, mechanism
  (none), electrics (none) and technique pass; **real_elements warns** about five rare parts,
  all chosen on purpose: two vintage parts of Captain Nemo's, and the diver's helmet, torso
  and legs, which came only in the 2012 Series 8 Diver (below).
- **What isn't drawn exactly**: the diver's brass helmet has no LDraw model, so brickkit draws
  its own approximation of it (a real part all the same: on the lists under its real number);
  his torso and legs prints have none either, so they are drawn plain in their colours. Every
  other print is LDraw's.

## The minifigures

Every head, torso and pair of legs is a real printed LEGO part, named by what LEGO,
Rebrickable and BrickLink sell: an assembly (a torso with its arms and hands, hips with legs)
rather than the loose LDraw pieces the model is drawn and posed with. The BrickLink numbers
are the ones the LDraw files' headers cite (`!KEYWORDS BrickLink ...`; for heads that is
`3626pb...` / `3626px...`, without the mould letter). "Sets" counts the sets the part came in
(through the minifigures in them), "last" the newest. A LEGO element ID puts the part in
`pick_a_brick.csv`, but Pick a Brick only sells what LEGO still makes; without one it is
BrickLink only. Skin: classic Yellow for all five, which most of the fitting prints (and the
striped torso) come in.

Both the head and the torso are older parts, and `real_elements` flags them as rare (fewer
than 3 sets or none since 2016). They are kept on purpose: the Boat Captain torso (Town

draws, and this head is the only stern, greying bearded face. Both are common on BrickLink.
For an all-current Nemo with no warnings, swap in torso `973c03h01pr1238` (black
double-breasted suit with gold buttons, but a red tie and an airline pin; 21 sets, 2020) and
head `3626cpr0933` (stubble goatee, raised eyebrow; 40 sets, 2026).

### A Nautilus diver (second from right, a step back)
One of Captain Nemo's crew in standard diving dress, as in the film's undersea scenes: the
brass deep-sea helmet with its round glass faceplate, a canvas-coloured diving suit with

- **The helmet** has no model in the LDraw library, so brickkit ships its own approximate one
  (`brickkit/data/ldraw/parts/10165c01.dat`, made by `tools/approximate_parts.py`; see the
  guide's "Real LEGO parts LDraw has no model of"). It is shaped from the real one's catalogue
  size (BrickLink: 1.85 x 1.8 x 2.1 cm, 1.96 g): a round brass dome over the head with a large
  round front faceplate (clear glass in a raised gold frame), a small port on each side, a
  valve on top, an air connector at the back and a bolted collar over the shoulders, 1.8 cm
  across. Its exact shape, its bolts and grilles are brickkit's guess, not LEGO's; it takes
  the head's stud like any hat. It is still the real part on the lists: `parts.csv` (note
  "approximate 3D model"), the BrickLink wanted list and Pick a Brick under 10165c01.
- **The suit**: LDraw has no model of the Series 8 Diver's torso or legs prints, so they are
  drawn plain (a Medium Nougat torso and legs, Dark Bluish Gray hips and hands, the colours of
  the real parts); the parts lists name the printed parts. Their BrickLink numbers aren't in
  any LDraw header, so they were looked up on bricklink.com and added to
  `brickkit/data/minifig_bricklink.json`. The original head (3626bpr1024) has no LDraw model
  either: a stand-in head would show a blank face through the faceplate, so the diver gets a
  common head LDraw draws.
- All three of the diver's parts were in one set, in 2012: `real_elements` flags them rare,
  and they are usually bought used on BrickLink.
- The speargun is held by its grip, upright at his side, out of everyone's way: with five
  figures four studs apart a forward-pointing gun would have reached past the deck's front
  edge, and the helmet's collar leaves less room for the arms.

Esmeralda the sea lion was the other candidate for a fifth figure, but LEGO's seal / sea lion
(`16765pr0001`, 3 Friends sets) has no LDraw model.

### Shopping
- **BrickLink**: upload `out/bricklink_wanted.xml`: every part, the figures' assemblies by
  their BrickLink numbers (all fifteen have one; none is left out).
- **BrickLink only**: Nemo's torso (no LEGO element ID; last in a set in 1991) and Ned Land's
  head (no element ID in Rebrickable's data). Nemo's head (last in a set in 2009) and the
  diver's helmet, torso and legs (2012) have element IDs but are long out of production:
  expect to find them on BrickLink, mostly used, not on Pick a Brick.
- **LEGO Pick a Brick**: `out/pick_a_brick.csv` lists everything with a LEGO element ID.
  Pick a Brick sells only what LEGO still makes and only some minifigure parts, so a few of
  these will come from BrickLink too.

## The base
16 x 8 studs: a black 8 x 16 plate under three reddish brown deck plates (6 x 8, 4 x 8,
6 x 8). On the deck (front to back): a row of gold 1 x 2 tiles along the front edge either
side of a **blank black 2 x 4 nameplate tile**; dark brown 1 x 6 walkway tiles; a row of bare
studs (the rivets, and what the figures stand on); reddish brown 2 x 2 deck plating; a round
dark brown **hatch** (round 4 x 4 plate) with a gold 2 x 2 round tile for its hand wheel at
the back left, and a mooring **bollard** (round 2 x 2 brick, gold top) at the back right.

The **bulkhead** (6 x 2, behind the middle of the crew) carries the **salon window**: three
courses of bricks with studs on one side, 40 LDU apart (a brick and two plates), face the
front; the window is built flat on a 6 x 6 plate (the round frame with its clear jumper and
inverted dish, eight Trans-Yellow round tiles on the frame's ring, tiles round the frame; its
four corner studs are left as rivets) and pressed onto them. Three curved 2 x 2 bricks round
off the top.

The five figures stand three studs apart (x = -120, -60, 0, 60, 120): Conseil, Aronnax,
Captain Nemo, the diver and Ned Land, left to right. Nemo, Conseil and Ned stand on row 2
(the row of rivets); Aronnax and the diver stand a row further back (row 3, where the deck
plating leaves their studs bare), so neighbours' hands and what they hold never meet. Nemo
stands in the middle, in front of his salon window. Conseil holds up a specimen bottle,
Aronnax holds his magnifying glass at his chest, Nemo his spyglass, the diver his speargun,
and Ned Land stands at the end with his harpoon upright, on the outside so it never hides a
face.

## Engine notes
This is the first model with minifigures (`model.minifig`, see the guide's "Minifigures"):
the figures are drawn from LDraw's separate pieces and posed (arms, hands, head), while the
parts lists, the BrickLink wanted list, the Pick a Brick list, the price and the
`real_elements` check use what is sold. The booklet builds each figure in its own section
(legs, torso, head, headwear, accessory) and shows the torso and legs as single pieces. It is
also the first model with an approximate 3D model of a real LEGO part (the diver's helmet).
