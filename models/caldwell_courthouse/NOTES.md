# The Caldwell County Courthouse: design notes

The 1894 Second Empire courthouse in Lockhart, Texas (Alfred Giles), as a large LEGO
display model: rough-faced buff stone with red trim, three storeys of tall windows under red
hoods, and the courthouse's roofline: **one central clock tower on a broad belfry, four bell
domes (two on the front facade and two on the back, standing on the dome pavilions and
flanking the tower), four corner pavilions under crested slate mansards**, and a steep slate
mansard with arched dormers, gabled attics and eight chimneys. A balconied entrance on the front
and back, columned porches on the sides. **The four clocks are real**: a quartz clock
insert (bought, not LEGO) sits behind each dial, and the tower's dome lifts off to set them.

- **6,659 LEGO pieces** in 160 part/colour lines, plus **4 quartz clock inserts** (not LEGO).
- **44.8 x 44.8 cm** base (56 x 56 studs, lawn and walks included), **65.4 cm** to the tip of
  the weather vane; the building is 48 x 48 studs over its projecting bays (38.4 cm) and
  its walls 19.8 cm tall. About 7.1 kg (estimated).
- **Price**: roughly $258-$862 for the LEGO parts (price bands, `out/price_estimate.md`)
  plus roughly $20-$60 for the four clock inserts (`out/hardware.csv`): about $278-$922.
- Every check passes (`out/report.html`): real elements, connections, collisions,
  buildability, stability, the mechanism sweep (hands turning, dome lifting), electrics
  (none) and technique. Colourway: reddish brown trim (`out/variants/brownstone/`).

## The plan (from the photos)

The model is scaled around the clock stage, which the four clock inserts fix at 11.6 studs
across: the building is about 4 times the tower's width (the real one about 7), so the
roofline can keep the photos' proportions. Each facade has seven bays, the rhythm of the
aerial view: a **corner pavilion** at each end (8 studs, projecting one stud), a **wing**
(4, set back), a **dome pavilion** (8, projecting) and the **entrance bay** in the middle
(8, set back, as wide as the belfry above it).

- **Tower**: a 10 x 10 belfry, as wide as the clock stage's core, rises about 20 cm from the
  roof with two tall dark arched openings under red hoods on each side (high up, level with
  the domes' tips) and red corner pilasters, then the clock stage, then the lift-off dome.
- **Bell domes**: on the front and back facades, each standing on its dome pavilion flush
  with the facade (the stage sits on the facade's cornice), so each facade shows two flanking
  the tower; the side facades have none. Each has a storey-tall 10 x 10 cream stage (0.86 of
  the clock stage's width; a stud wider than the pavilion on each side) with red corner
  pilasters and red bands at the top, a big round window (6 studs across, a red frame round
  dark glass) on its front and sides, a red cornice a stud wider with a little pediment over
  each window, then a slate bell about as tall as it is wide: a 45-degree flared foot, a
  steep 75-degree body, two gentler stages swelling to a short point, red ribs up every
  corner and a red finial on a black rod. The finials reach the belfry's openings, below the
  clock stage.
- **Corner pavilions**: slate mansards over the whole 8-stud pavilion, a steep 75-degree
  slope with a tall dormer on each outer face rising to a flat 6 x 6 top crowned with iron
  cresting; clearly lower than the domes.
- **Main roof**: a steep 75-degree slate mansard (3 bricks tall) with arched dormers (round-topped windows with dark lattice panes) over the wings
  (and, on the sides, over the dome pavilions), a gabled attic over each entrance (over the
  porch on the sides), and eight tall cream chimneys with red caps rising above it. Next to
  each dome, a low 45-degree slope keeps clear of the dome's side window.

## Build order (booklet sections)

1. **Base**: two layers of plates, green lawn, grey walks with tiles.
2. **Columns** (x8): 2 x 2 grey columns from the base to the roof deck under the belfry's
   corners and the four domes.
3. **Walls**, as segments that stand on the base: a **corner pavilion** (an L of two 8-stud
   faces, x4), a **wing** (x8), a **dome pavilion** (x8) and an **entrance bay** (x4: front
   and back with a balcony, the sides without). Rough-faced tan courses (1 x 2 and 1 x 4
   masonry-profile bricks) on a plinth, red bands and belt courses; windows on three floors
   (1 x 2 x 3, 1 x 2 x 3, 1 x 2 x 2), each under a red 1 x 4 arch as a segmental hood. The
   entrance: a glazed double door under a red 1 x 6 x 2 arch, a pair of windows under
   another, two windows above; on the front and back the belt course steps out one stud as
   a balcony with an iron railing.
4. **Roof deck and cornice**: two layers of plates over the whole building (tan then red at
   the edge) tie the segments together; red inverted slopes, plates and tiles make the
   overhanging cornice.
5. **Mansards**: steep slopes, arched dormers, attics and chimneys; bricks and plates where
   the domes' stages will stand; slate tiles on the flat roof.
6. **Corner pavilion roofs**, built in place on the cornice.
7. **Belfry** (10 x 10, about 20 cm).
8. **Bell domes** (x4, one sub-assembly placed four times).
9. **Clock stage** with its four **clock faces** (built flat) and the **clock inserts**.
10. **Lift-off top**: tan frieze, red cornice, the bell-shaped dome with red ribs and a star
    medallion on each face, a red finial and the weather vane.
11. **Side porches**, **shrubs**, **live oaks**, **flagpoles**.

## The clocks

### What to buy

Four **"mini" quartz clock inserts** of the kind sold for a **1-3/8 inch (35 mm) hole**
(press-in / fit-up inserts for woodworking and photo frames, often called "1-7/16 inch
mini clock insert" after the bezel). Search for "1-3/8 inch mini clock insert" or "35 mm
clock insert": clock-making and woodworking suppliers (Klockit, Rockler, Woodcraft), craft
stores, Amazon or eBay; roughly $5-$15 each. Choose a **white dial with black numerals**
(Roman numerals match the courthouse). The bezel colour doesn't matter much: only a thin
ring of it shows round the dial (black looks best). Each needs a button cell, usually
included (SR626SW / 377 or LR44).

### How to check the size

Measure before you buy (or check the listing):

| | fits | the model was drawn with |
|---|---|---|
| body (the part behind the bezel) | 35 mm across or less | 35 mm |
| bezel | 34-40 mm across | 38 mm |
| depth, front of the bezel to the back of the body | 22 mm or less | 17.6 mm |
| setting knob | may stick out a few mm more | 2.4 mm (20 mm overall) |
| dial | shows whole up to about 31 mm | 30.4 mm |

Lay the insert face down on the table and measure how high the back of the body stands.

### How they fit

Each clock face is a SNOT panel: plates with their studs outward, carrying a red ring (four
4 x 4 macaroni tiles) round a white ring (four 3 x 3 macaroni tiles) with a **32 mm round
window**. Behind the window the plates leave a 4 x 4 stud (32 mm) square hole. The insert's
bezel is bigger than that, so it sits **behind** the plates and only the dial shows through
the window, framed by the white and red rings, like the real clock faces.

Behind each face the clock stage has a **slot open to the top**: the insert slides down it
face first, its bezel behind the face plates, its body resting on **two tile rails 16 mm
apart**. A round body on two rails centres itself, so a 35 mm body sits within half a
millimetre of the window's centre (a smaller one sits a little lower). The slot is 48 mm
wide and the floor under the bezel leaves room for bezels up to 40 mm. The four inserts sit back to back: their faces are 40 mm
from the tower's axis, which leaves room for four bodies up to 22 mm deep before they would
meet in the corners, and a 45 mm square shaft between their backs.

The **lift-off top** (frieze, cornice, dome, vane) sits on four single studs, one on top of
each corner column of the clock stage, and covers the four slots, so the inserts can't lift
out while it is on. Lift it straight up: the backs of all four inserts, with their setting
knobs and battery covers, face the open middle of the tower. Set the time with a fingertip
or a pencil's eraser; to change a battery lift the insert straight out of its slot. If an
insert slides back in its slot when the model is handled, a small square of foam tape
behind it keeps its bezel against the face.

### In the model and the checks

The inserts are stand-in parts from brickkit's new non-LEGO hardware support
(`docs/brickkit-guide.md`, "Non-LEGO hardware"): `bk-clock-insert-35mm` (bezel, white dial
with hour marks, 35 mm body, setting knob, drawn at the largest size the slots take) with
separate `bk-clock-hand-hour` and `bk-clock-hand-minute`, all under
`brickkit/data/ldraw/parts/` (made by `tools/hardware_parts.py`). They show in the renders,
the booklet and the viewer; the collision check sees them (the slots, rails and faces
really have room); `model.press_fit` makes the connections and buildability checks treat
each insert as held by what it touches (rails and face plates) and each hand as held on its
insert's spindle. They are not on the LEGO parts lists; `out/hardware.csv`, the price
estimate's "Not LEGO" section and the booklet's "You will need" list them with where to
buy them. The eight hands are moving groups and the top is a `lifts_off` group: pose(t)
turns the hands from 10:10 to 12:10 and lifts the dome 8 cm from t = 0.35.

## Techniques

- **Walls** are one stud thick, courses bonded with staggered joints; the corners of an L
  alternate between its two walls course by course. The rough stone is Tan
  masonry-profile bricks (98283, 15533) turned with their profile outward. Window frames
  stand in the courses; their glass clicks in from behind. 60602 (glass for the 1 x 2 x 3
  window) had no LDCad snap, so `brickkit/data/shadow/parts/60602.dat` gives it the finger
  its frame's glazing slot expects.
- **SNOT**: the clock faces, oculi and star medallions are built lying flat and pressed onto
  bricks with studs on the side (1 x 4, 1 x 1 and 1 x 1 on two adjacent sides). The clock
  faces' columns of plates overhang the tower's core by one stud at their right-hand end,
  pinwheel fashion, so each corner is covered by exactly one face.
- **Round shapes from macaroni tiles**: 4 x 4 and 3 x 3 macaroni tiles for the clock rings;
  quarter tiles tangent to the ring fill the face corners. The domes' round windows: a
  6 x 6 round plate, four 3 x 3 quarter-ring plates (68568) as the red frame (their studs
  show, like a beaded moulding; a Dark Red 3 x 3 macaroni tile is too rare), and 2 x 2
  macaroni and quarter tiles as the dark glass inside it.
- **Bell domes**: the stage stands on the facade's cornice (its two outer front cells on the
  cornice's overhang, whose tiles are left off there; bricks and plates bring the roof deck
  up to the same height under the rest of its ring); the cornice plates each rest on the
  stage's top ring and overhang one stud. The bell is built like the tower's dome, one stud
  smaller: each ring of slopes steps in one stud (45-degree flare, 75-degree body, two
  45-degree rings, a 2 x 2 quadruple-convex point), red double-convex corners as ribs, and
  a red cone on a black bar in the point's top hole as the finial.
- **Star medallions**: a jumper plate on two side studs puts a single stud in the middle of
  each dome face; a tan 2 x 2 dish sits on it and a red star on the dish's stud.
- **Tower dome**: each stage of slopes steps in one stud all round, so its toe sits on the
  previous stage's back row and the slopes run on without ledges: a 45-degree flare at the
  foot, two 75-degree stages, two 45-degree stages and a 75-degree point (concave at the
  foot, swelling to the point), with red double-convex corners up every corner as ribs. A
  red cone threaded on the vane's bar is the finial.
- **Mansards**: steep 75-degree slopes along the facades with arched dormers (1 x 2 x 2 2/3
  round-topped windows whose lattice panes clip in from behind, before the slate cheeks
  go on); on the corner pavilions one ring of 75-degree slopes (slate double-convex
  corners) to a flat 6 x 6 top, dormers whose little gables are two cheese slopes, iron
  cresting of 1 x 4 x 2 railings and round posts with cones.
- **Cornices**: red inverted slopes on the wall top, plates over them and the overhang,
  tiles on the edge; the convex corners are covered by cantilevered plates.
- **Colours**: the trim is Dark Red, chosen by rendering Dark Red, Reddish Brown, Red and
  Dark Orange against the photos (Reddish Brown reads brown, Red too bright, Dark Orange
  orange-brown); the colourway keeps Reddish Brown. The walls are Tan. The slate is Black:
  Dark Bluish Gray rendered mid-grey next to the photos' charcoal slate. Cresting, spikes
  and the vane are Black too.

## Known limits

- **Not built with real bricks.** The checks cover part existence, connections,
  collisions, every insertion, balance and the mechanism sweep, not clutch or handling.
  The model is heavy (about 7.1 kg) on a two-layer plate base: lift it from underneath,
  and support the roof deck (it spans the whole building on eight hidden columns) when
  pressing parts on from above.
- **Clock inserts vary.** The slots take the range in the table above; the stand-in is the
  largest insert of that kind. Nothing but friction and the lift-off top keeps an insert
  forward in its slot (see the foam tip). The dial sits about 7.5 mm behind the face of the
  red ring, set back like a porthole.
- **Size.** 65.4 cm on a 44.8 cm base: the clock stage is 11.6 studs across its faces (four
  inserts up to 20 mm deep back to back need their faces at least 3.5 cm from the axis), and
  the building is scaled to 48 studs so the tower, domes and pavilions keep the photos'
  proportions; the belfry is tall enough for the domes' tips to reach its openings. The tower is still relatively wider than the real one (the building is about
  4 times its width, the real one about 7). The base is 56 studs (not 52) to fit the side
  porches and a lawn in front.
- **Simplified.** The four facades share one wall design apart from the front and back
  balconies and the side porches; the real facades' quoins, paired pilasters, carved hoods
  and triple windows are simplified to single windows under red arches. The bells are made
  of straight slopes (their curve is a polygon), and their red ribs are two studs wide
  (corner slopes); the tower's dome is built the same way. The domes' stages are a stud
  wider than their pavilions on each side.
- The flags are plain (red, blue, dark blue) wavy flags, no prints. The live oaks are
  small (the lawn is only a few studs deep). Proportions follow the photos, not a survey.
- Interior: hollow, with the eight columns inside; the windows show dark rooms.
