# Stormtrooper PEZ

A Quick Bricks model: 56 pieces, 10.6 cm tall, about the size of a real PEZ dispenser. It is a
white Stormtrooper helmet (two black eyes, a black brow, a frown, grey vents) on a white stem
with a small foot, and the head tips back on a click hinge to show a pink sweet, as a real one
does. The head is 3 cm wide and the foot 1.6 cm by 3.2 cm. It is our own design, made from two
pictures of a real dispenser, and one of three PEZ models on the same stem
(`brickkit/designs/pez.py`; the others are `pez_darth_vader` and `pez_yoda`). All 8 checks
pass; the stability check's limit is lowered from 10 degrees to 6 (see below).

## Source

- **From:** two pictures of a real Stormtrooper PEZ dispenser, given by the owner in
  `inbox/pez-stormtrooper/`. They show the real thing, not a brick model, so there are no
  building steps to copy. The owner asked for the three "as close to real life size as
  possible", each on "the same base, in the correct color", with faces "as close as you can
  get" and heads that "hinge like a real pez".
- **Designer / credit:** our own design, by lead (Claude) to the owner's brief. No brick model
  was copied, so no credit line is needed. Who took the pictures is not known.
- **Saved in `reference/`:** `picture_01.jpeg`: the dispenser from the front on white, with a
  Star Wars logo in the corner; it shows the black band over the eyes, the frown and the marks
  at the chin. `picture_02.jpeg`: a real one from the side and a little above; it shows the
  helmet in profile, the raised PEZ letters on the stem and the thin foot. Both are local only:
  git ignores `models/pez_*/reference/`. The originals stay in the inbox.
- **Taken in:** 2026-10-10 by lead (Claude).

## What had to be worked out

Everything: the pictures show a moulded plastic toy, and the bricks are ours. These are the
choices, and what is not like the real thing.

- **The size.** A real dispenser is about 10.5 cm tall with its head. That is the figure the
  design was made to; the pictures carry no scale and no dispenser was measured. Ours is 264
  LDU: 10.6 cm. The foot is a 2 x 4 plate, 1.6 cm by 3.2 cm, long from front to back. The stem
  is one stud wide and two deep and, with its tiles, 14.4 mm by 16 mm and 6.4 cm tall. The
  head is three studs square at its core, 2.9 cm tall and 3 cm wide over the cheeks.
- **The stem.** Four rounds of two 1 x 2 plates and two bricks with a stud on each side
  (47905), and two 2 x 4 tiles standing on those studs each side for the smooth sides. Two
  jumper plates centre the one-stud stem on the two-stud foot. It is solid: there is no
  magazine and no spring.
- **The sweet.** One 1 x 2 tile on top of the stem, which does not come out. The head rests
  on it when shut, and it shows when the head is tipped back. Its colour (Bright Pink) is our
  choice; the pictures show no sweets.
- **The hinge.** Two locking hinge plates: the one with two fingers (54657) flat on top of the
  stem, its fingers past the back, and the one with one finger (44301b) upright on the back of
  the head, finger down. They click every 22.5 degrees, and the design tips the head back
  three clicks: 67.5 degrees. The mechanism check sweeps 24 poses of it and finds nothing in
  the way. The head is held by the hinge alone; the sweet under it is a tile and grips
  nothing.
- **Which hinge plates.** The older moulds of the same pair are dated: in White, 44301a was
  last in a set in 2013 and 44302a in 2020. 44301b and 54657 are in sets up to 2026 (45 and
  27 sets in White).
- **The head's core.** Eight tall bricks with two studs on one side (32952) in a ring, hollow
  in the middle, tied by the 3 x 3 plate on top. Three face the front and carry the face, two
  face out on each side, and one faces back for the hinge. Each side's two bricks are joined
  first by what goes on their studs (a cheek), so nothing stands loose while the head is
  built.
- **What in the face stands for what.** The two black quarter-round tiles, round corners down
  and out, are the helmet's lenses. The black 1 x 3 plate above them is the black band across
  the brow. The black half circle, round side up, is the frown. The two grey round tiles
  beside it and the grey grille tile low on each cheek are the helmet's vents. The lower half
  of the face stands one plate in front of the eyes, like the helmet's snout.
- **What could not be done at this size.** In the pictures the frown is a thin line and the
  lenses run straight into the black band. Here the frown is one tile 8 mm wide, and a white
  strip one plate high (the edge of the 3 x 3 plate) shows between the eyes and the brow.
  The small marks on the real helmet are reduced to the round tiles and the grilles. The
  helmet is a box with a low dome, not the rounded shape of the real one. The stem's raised
  PEZ letters are left out, and the foot is two plates thick where the real one is thin.
- **The dome's slopes.** A curved slope (11477) has a cut-out one plate high under its thick
  end. Each of the four so holds by the one stud under its thin end, and the stud under its
  thick end shows in the cut-out: a small notch in the middle of each side of the dome. At the
  front that stud belongs to the black brow plate, so it shows as a dark dot above the brow in
  the stills (`out/renders/close_head.png`).
- **Does it stand?** Like the real thing, only just: it is tall on a small foot. It weighs
  about 30 g and tips to the side at 7.4 degrees, the foot being two studs wide (the check
  wants at least 10). So `[checks.stability] min_tilt_deg = 6` in `model.toml`, with a
  comment.
- **Not tried in bricks.** The checks are on the computer model: 95 connections (94 stud, 1
  hinge) in one assembly, no overlaps, every piece with a clear way on. No physical build is
  recorded, so whether the hinge holds the head up when it is tipped back is not known.

## Parts and price

- 56 pieces in 21 lines. Every line has a LEGO element ID; none is flagged rare.
  `out/parts.csv`, `out/pick_a_brick.csv` and `out/bricklink_wanted.xml` are ready to upload.

## Quick Bricks

- `[quick]` in `model.toml`: the dark blue mat of white dots (`blue_mat`: stars) in the night
  room, daylight (the warm evening light turned the white cream), 18 seconds, clicks only.
- `highlight = ["eyes", "frown", "sweet"]`, three close-ups: the sweet as it goes on the stem,
  then the frown and an eye as the head is built.
- `flex = 2.8`: in the last seconds before the cut the head tips back on its hinge (the
  mechanism's own pose, eased), holds while the sweet shows, and shuts again, so the video
  ends on the face.
- With 56 pieces the parts come in from off frame (the opening with the parts laid out is for
  models under 25).
- The video's build plan is clean, audited the way `brickkit status pez_stormtrooper --check`
  does it: no piece passes through another and each lands on something.
- `out/renders_open/three_quarter.png` is a still with the head tipped back and the sweet
  showing.
- The final video was not rendered when this was written; `brickkit status` shows where it is.
  `collection = "quick_bricks"` is not set: it is not on the site.
