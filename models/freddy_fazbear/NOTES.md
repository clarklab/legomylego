# Freddy Fazbear

A Quick Bricks model: 42 pieces in 25 lines, about 9 cm tall, 3.4 cm wide (arm to arm) and 4 cm
deep. A brown bear robot with a black top hat and bow tie, big round eyes, a light snout and
nose, round ears, and a silver microphone in its right hand. It is Rebrickable MOC-265184
(https://rebrickable.com/mocs/MOC-265184/), built from a 32-page set of plans, one numbered step
per page. `design.py` has one step per page (32 steps); all 8 checks pass and the Quick Bricks
build audit (`brickkit status --check`) says clean. Three steps are in a different order from
the pages, and a few parts differ from the pages (see *What had to be worked out*).

## Source

  page images. Each page has one big step number, a box of the parts to add (with quantities),

How the table names things. "Left" and "right" are the bear's own: facing the bear, its left is
on your right. The plans' camera looks at the bear from the front and its left side, so the
bear's right arm is on the left of the pictures. A *row* is one stud (20 LDU, 8 mm) tall;
"front" is the way the bear faces. Most of the body is built sideways: plates with their studs
facing front. "Layer" means how far back a plate lies, front to back. Part numbers are LEGO's

## What had to be worked out

Pictures only (no LDraw or Studio file), so every position was found from the stud rows in the
pictures and then checked against the snap data (`brickkit all`). How sure each one is:

- **Frame and layers.** The legs stand on the feet's back studs. The hip is a sandwich, front
  to back: a 2 x 2 plate, a gap one plate thick holding the two handle plates (bottom row) and
  the rounded 1 x 2 plate (middle row), then the 2 x 3 plate. Found from where the 2 x 2's
  studs sit against the 2 x 3's (exactly 16 LDU in front of them). Very sure.
- **Steps 6, 10, 11: a different order from the pages.** The pages put the 2 x 2 plate on at
  step 6 and the rounded plate and the second clip plate into the gap afterwards. That cannot
  be built: the gap is closed and the 2 x 3's studs stand in it. Here the 2 x 3 plate goes on
  alone at step 6, the second handle plate at step 7 as the page says, the rounded plate at
  step 10 (the page has it at 11), and the 2 x 2 plate and the belly tile at step 11 (the pages
  have them at 6 and 10). The finished model is the same.
- **Steps 5 and 7: the clip plates.** The pages show a 1 x 1 plate with a horizontal clip,
  hooked into the leg's clip. In Reddish Brown that part (61252) was only in 2 sets, last in
  2018 (6019 only in 2 sets, last in 2004), and brickkit's snap data has no clip hooking into
  a clip, so the leg would count as not joined to the body and the video could not build it.
  Built here with the common Plate Special 1 x 1 Rounded with Handle (26047, 182 sets, still
  made): the leg's clip grips its handle bar, a real clip connection, and it does the same job.
  It is mostly hidden in the gap. Fairly sure of what the page shows, a deliberate swap.
- **Step 4/8: Bar Holder with Clip (11090).** Matched by its shape (a short round shaft, a clip
  with its mouth up, a hole in its side) and it fits over the pin of the step 3 tile. Sure.
- **Step 3/9: the tile with a pin (20482).** A flat round tile with a thin pin on top, the pin
  the holder fits over. Sure. Step 2 and step 9's round plate have a solid stud (6141); the ears
  (steps 29, 30) have an open stud (85861): the callouts show the difference.
- **Step 10: the belly tile is Tan, not Light Nougat.** The page shows a pale peach round 2 x 2
  tile. A Light Nougat round 2 x 2 tile was never made (4150 and 14769 are not made in it), so
  it is Tan, the nearest colour with the part (14769, 121 sets). The notch at its edge in the
  callout is why it is 14769 and not 4150 (unsure). The muzzle (step 24) is Light Nougat as the
  page shows, so the belly and the muzzle are slightly different pale colours here.
- **Step 18: the neck plate is Black.** The 1 x 2 plate with two horizontal clips (60470) was
  never made in Reddish Brown (only in Dark Brown, 5 sets, last 2019: rare). Black has 592
  sets, still made. It is hidden by the bow tie and the head. If a Dark Brown one turns up it is
  a straight swap (`neck` in `model.toml`).
- **Steps 13 to 16: arms and hands.** The arm is the 98313 mechanical arm (its clip on the
  shoulder bar, a rod hole at the end of the forearm). The pose is a guess from the pictures:
  the left forearm swung 40 degrees down from straight forward, the right (microphone) arm 15
  degrees; the angle can be changed with `ARM_RELAXED` and `ARM_MIC` in `design.py`. The hand
  is a Bar 1.5L with Clip (3484, Full Clip); the page could also be 48729b (a gap in its clip),
  and I could not tell. The hand's bar goes 9 LDU into the rod hole.
- **Step 17: the microphone.** A silver half-sphere top on a black handle: 90370pr0007
  (LDraw `90370p05`), Black. The plain microphone (90370) in Black or Light Bluish Gray would
  also be real. The 15-degree arm tilts the microphone forward by 15 degrees too; the pictures
  look upright.
- **Steps 19, 20: the bow tie.** Sure of the parts. The two cheese slopes' slanted faces fall
  towards the middle (wings that flare out); fairly sure.
- **Steps 21 to 23: the head's layers.** From the front: nose, muzzle, 1 x 2 plate, 1 x 2 plate,
  the handle plate (48336), the 2 x 2 plate, the inverted slope. The handle plate's handle is in
  the neck's two clips. Very sure of the plates (the studs of each layer measured against the
  next: 8 LDU apart). 48336 (closed ends) rather than 2540 (free ends): the callout shows end
  caps.
- **Step 23: the inverted slope.** The callout is an inverted 45-degree 2 x 2 slope. brickkit
  maps 3660 to an obsolete mould that is not made in Reddish Brown, so it is 76959, the same
  shape. In the picture it is behind the 2 x 2 plate, level with its top, its slanted face at
  the back and to the right. Here its four studs go into the 2 x 2 plate's four sockets and
  the slope faces back and down. **Not sure:** the picture shows its open underside facing
  the camera, which I could not explain with this part and these neighbours.
- **Step 27: the extra 1 x 2 plate.** The callout lists a 1 x 2 plate with the bracket. I could
  not find where it goes in the picture (the red outline reaches the back right of the head).
  Here it is on the back of the inverted slope, its studs in the slope's two underside
  sockets, at the top row. **Guess.** It is the one part whose place I am not sure of.
- **Step 27: the bracket.** The 1 x 2 leaf sits on the two ear-post plates' hollow studs, so its
  front studs are 8 LDU in front of theirs (measured), and its 2 x 2 leaf is the roof with four
  studs. Sure.
- **Steps 29, 30: the ears.** The round plate with an open stud is on the end of the post (the
  post's tip goes into the hole under it); the picture shows the plate's centre at the post's tip.
  Fairly sure; the plate is 10 LDU from the head.
- **Steps 31, 32: the hat.** The brim is on the roof's four studs; the round brick is on its
  back stud (three studs show in the picture). Fairly sure of which stud.
- **Colours.** The brown looks like Reddish Brown: every part exists in it except the ones
  noted above. White eyes, Black bow tie, nose and hat.
- **Stability.** He is 9 cm tall on a 16 mm square; he stands (the centre of mass is 74% of the
  way to the middle of the feet) but a perfectly centred one would tip at only about 9 degrees,
  so `[checks.stability] min_tilt_deg = 6` in `model.toml` instead of the default 10, with a
  comment. The check is still on.
