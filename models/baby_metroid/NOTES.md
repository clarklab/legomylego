# Baby Metroid: design notes

Life-size larval Metroid (Super Metroid), about 25 cm across, hovering on a clear stand.
2,679 pieces in 64 part/colour lines, about 1.5 kg (estimated). Every check passes except one
warning: the Power Functions battery box is rare (last in a set in 2015).

## What works

- **Fangs**: turning the knob at the back opens and closes all four fangs together. A worm
  drives a 24-tooth gear on the +X fang's axle. Each fang's axle carries an inward lever, and
  thick 1x5 links tie the four levers to a hub under the body. When the driven fang turns, the
  hub moves up or down and turns the other three fangs. The worm is self-locking, so the fangs
  hold any position. A 12-tooth bevel pair turns the worm's vertical axle into the horizontal
  knob axle. The mechanism check sweeps 24 poses over the 45-degree range with no collisions.
- **Nuclei**: three hollow Trans-Red balls, each lit from below by a Power Functions LED
  (8870). The LED is pushed up into the centre pin hole of a Dark Red round 4x4 plate (60474).
- **Hover stand**: 8 Trans-Clear round bricks on an axle 16. The column plugs into a black
  socket under the body, so the Metroid lifts off. The round base hides the battery box. A
  hatch in the top exposes the switch and the output plug.

## Wiring (electrics check)

- 2x 8870 light units: one for the two front nuclei, one for the back nucleus. The second
  lamp of the back unit is not used; tuck it under the membrane.
- The leads are laid before the membrane is built. Each plug goes down through the 3x2 opening
  in the base disc (x -120..-60, z 40..80), next to the back-left post. Under the membrane the
  leads run in the 16 LDU (6.4 mm) gap above the hub. The hub only moves down, so the gap never
  closes. Below the body the leads drop straight to the hatch in the stand and plug onto the
  battery box.
- **Assumption**: LEGO lists the 8870 lead as 50 cm. I could not confirm where it splits to
  its two lamps, so each lamp is budgeted 40 cm. The longest run needs about 23 cm.
- To lift the Metroid off its stand, unplug the leads at the hatch first.

## Decisions

- Dome: Trans-Light Blue. It has the most slope, brick and plate shapes of the pale
  transparent colours.
- Skirt: Coral, with a Dark Red base disc and knob bearing.
- Fangs: White bent beams (32348) with two tooth pieces (41669) each. The spec's Light Nougat
  fang base was dropped; the whole fang is White.
- Inner body: a Dark Red woven membrane on three black posts. It carries the nuclei and hides
  the mechanism, and stands in for the spec's ribbed "mouth" structure.
- Back nucleus: raised on a hollow chimney of Dark Red bricks, so its lamp's lead runs down
  inside it.

## Known limits

- Not built with real bricks. The checks cover part existence, connections, collisions,
  every insertion, balance, the mechanism sweep and lead lengths. They cannot judge clutch,
  part tolerances, how stiff the axle-16 column is, or how the dome survives handling.
- The part count is well above the 900-1,400 first estimated. The woven dome and the stand
  base account for most of it. A lower count would need a coarser dome.
- Power Functions was discontinued in 2018. The 8870 lights and the 64228 AAA battery box
  (set 88000) have to be bought used. The Powered Up equivalents (the 88005 Light and a
  Powered Up hub) have different plugs and a different box size, and this design has not been
  checked with them.
- Through the faceted dome, the three nuclei read as red glows more than as three separate
  balls.
