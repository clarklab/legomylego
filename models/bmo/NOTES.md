# BMO

A Quick Bricks model: BMO, the little living game console from Adventure Time, in 44 pieces. It
is 3 cm wide, 2.4 cm deep and 4.8 cm tall: a teal box on two short legs, with a pale green
screen (two dot eyes and a smile), a slot, a yellow D-pad, a blue triangle, a green and a red
button, and two arms that swing. Our own design, made from four pictures of the character. The
owner asked for it "as small as you can while still getting decent detail", because BMO is
small beside Finn and Jake. All 8 checks pass.

## What had to be worked out

- **The size.** The screen has to have a teal frame, and a frame one stud wide would make BMO
  five studs across. So the frame is one plate thick: the core is three studs wide, the screen
  is the full three, and tiles on the core's side studs are the frame. They are three studs
  deep where the core and the panels together are 2.8, so they stand 1.6 mm proud of the face,
  like the real one's inset screen. The lid is three tiles on top; it is as wide as the core,
  so the top corners step in by a plate (BMO's are rounded).
- **The face.** A cell of the screen is one stud, and a plain black round tile for an eye fills
  a sixth of the screen: the first try looked like a chessboard. The eyes are white round
  tiles printed with a small black dot off the middle, turned so the dots are towards each
  other, and the mouth is the print with one thin curved line, turned to smile. Their white
  is close to the Light Aqua of the screen, so they read as dots and a line on the screen.
- **The buttons.** Two rows of three: the slot (a grille tile) and the green button, then the
  D-pad, the triangle and the red button. Left out: the small dark blue button and the two
  dashes at the bottom left. The D-pad is a yellow 1 x 1 plate, not a cross: the 1 x 1 flower
  plate with four petals would be closer but is wider than a stud (it would foul its
  neighbours) and has not been made in yellow since 2004. The triangle is a cheese slope seen
  from the front. The slot is black: the teal grille tile was last made in 2002.
- **The arms.** Each is a curved slope on the one stud of a 2 x 2 jumper in the side, thick end
  down like a mitten, so it turns: BMO can wave. In the model they hang straight down.
- **The feet.** 1 x 3 plates, front to back, bigger than BMO's: on 1 x 2 plates it tipped
  backwards at 7 degrees.
- **Inside.** The four bricks with studs on two sides are Light Bluish Gray (they do not come
  in teal). They are hidden by the panels, the side tiles and the plates above and below.
- **Not done:** the letters "BMO" and the speaker holes on his left side.

## Parts and price

44 pieces in 20 lines, all with a LEGO element ID, none rare. Roughly $2 to $5 on BrickLink
(`out/price_estimate.md`, a rough range, not live prices). The two printed tiles are ordinary
ones (98138pr0273 and 98138pr9977 on Rebrickable).
