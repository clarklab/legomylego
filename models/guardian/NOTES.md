# Guardian

Our own model of a Guardian, the six-legged ancient machine from The Legend of Zelda: 183
pieces, 25.6 cm across its legs, 22 cm front to back and 13.7 cm tall. A round stone-grey body
with a dome, a head with one blue eye, pink lights, and six legs on small ball joints that can
be posed. The body stands on a stack of four black round bricks, so the legs only have to hold
their pose.

## Source

- **From:** two pictures the owner pasted into the chat on 2026-10-10, with the brief
  "Guardian from Zelda Tears of the Kingdom, under 300 pieces". `picture_01.jpg` is a painted
  illustration of a Guardian firing its beam, seen three-quarters on; `picture_02.jpg` is a
  screenshot of one walking, seen from the front and a little below.
- **Design:** ours. No LEGO model was copied; nothing here is an official set.
- **Kept in `reference/`, local only:** both pictures. They are the game's art, so git
  ignores them; the originals are in `inbox/guardian/`.
- **Taken in:** 2026-10-10 by lead.

## What had to be worked out

- **Where six legs can go.** A round 8 x 8 plate has studs for a 1 x 2 plate at its edge in
  only six places that leave a socket clear of the rim: the middle of each side (studs at
  70, ±10) and two at the front and two at the back (studs at 30 and 50, ±50). Those six are
  within a degree of a regular hexagon: 0, 60, 120 degrees and so on. So the legs are evenly
  spaced, like the machine's.
- **Clearance for the sockets.** A socket's cup stands 4 LDU above and below its plate. Each
  socket plate therefore has a black 1 x 2 plate under it and another over it, which makes
  the band between the rims exactly one brick high and keeps both rims a plate clear of the
  cup. A socket with its two plates is a sub-assembly, made six times.
- **The legs' shape.** Each leg is five links 60 LDU long and a foot. Their pitches from
  the body outwards are 18, 40, -4.7, -52 and -80 degrees, and the foot is at -88: up over
  an arch a little higher than the top of the dome, then straight down. No joint is bent more than 48
  degrees. The front and back legs leave their sockets 30 degrees to the side as well, to
  point straight out from the middle. The part shapes do not touch at any joint; whether a
  real joint is comfortable at these angles has not been tried.
- **The stand.** Six legs on small friction ball joints would each have to carry about 10 g
  at the end of a 9 cm arm. By our estimate (not a measurement) that is about as much as one
  of these joints holds, with nothing to spare, so the body does not hang on the legs: it stands on four black round 2 x 2 bricks
  under its middle. (Clear ones would hide the stand better on a real desk, and fit the same
  way; in the video four clear bricks stalled the renderer, so the model's own are black.) The third link's pitch (-4.7) was found so that all six claws touch the
  table with the body at that height.
- **Legs one stud wide.** The joint plates are 1 x 2, so the legs are one stud wide. A black
  1 x 2 plate between each joint plate and its white armour makes them as deep as they are
  wide. Putting that plate underneath instead (dark below, as in the pictures) would have
  meant pressing 30 plates on from below; on top, every piece of a leg goes on from above.
- **The dome is plain.** The quarter domes have one stud each, at the top, so nothing can be
  put on the dome's sides: the swirls and pink lines of the pictures are only suggested, by
  the tan ring at the top, the slopes at the foot and the lights round the band. The eight
  studs of the rim nearest the dome stay bare: the dome leaves no room for a piece on them.
- **One claw, not three.** A plate with three teeth points them sideways from a leg this
  narrow, so each foot has one tooth, pointing down.
- **The eye on a half pin.** The round brick's side holes take a half pin, whose stud holds
  the eye's round plate by its middle. The engine finds all eight of the head's pin joints
  and the studs on them.

## Checks

- `brickkit all guardian`: all eight checks pass, with none switched off and no contact
  allowed. 396 connections (345 stud, 42 ball, 8 pin, 1 axle), one assembly, no overlaps,
  every piece has a clear way on. It weighs about 123 g and tips at 59 degrees.
- `brickkit status guardian --check`: the video's build plan is clean. For that the planner
  learned one thing (brickkit/video/assemble.py): a ball pushed into its socket the way a
  hint says may brush the socket's lip in the last 8 LDU. No other model has ball joints, so
  no other plan changed.
- Checked on a computer only. Nobody has built it with real bricks yet, and the legs are
  the part most likely to need changing when somebody does.

## Quick Bricks

- `[quick]` in `model.toml`: 35 s (183 pieces, and the body, the head, six sockets and six
  legs are built beside the model and joined), clicks only.
- **The legs move at the end** (`flex = 3.0`, the owner's request): for the last three seconds
  before the cut the Guardian walks on the spot. `design.py`'s `model.pose` turns each leg in
  its socket, about its ball: three legs lift (front right, left, back right), then the other
  three, twice each, and every claw comes down where it started. A corner leg already stands
  as far out of line with its socket as the cup allows (the socket faces front or back, the
  leg points out from the middle: about 30 degrees round and 18 up), so it can lift 3 degrees
  at most where it stands; swung 8 degrees back into line it can lift 18. So the corner legs
  step inwards as they lift (12 degrees round, 15 up) and the side legs swing to and fro
  (9 degrees, 15 up). The mechanism check sweeps the walk in 24 poses and the video plan's
  audit checks every frame of it: nothing touches.
- The scene: the green LEGO floor, the window room, daylight. On the grey baseplate in the
  white studio (the first preview) the grey and white machine washed out; on green it reads
  as standing in a field.
- Close-ups: a claw (tag `claw`, as the left leg goes on) and the eye (tag `eye`).
