# Xenomorph

A Quick Bricks model: 51 pieces, all Black but one Flat Silver round plate for the teeth and
an Orange cat riding on its back. It is 10.8 cm from its claws to the tip of its tail, 2.3 cm
wide and 7.8 cm tall to the cat's ears (6.7 cm without the cat). Every joint moves: the legs,
arms, head and back tubes hang on clips and bars, the tail on click hinges. It stands on its
two feet and the barb at the end of its tail.

The design is another builder's: **yodakya**, Rebrickable
[MOC-34927](https://rebrickable.com/mocs/MOC-34927/). This folder is that model, part for part,
posed so that its joints close and it stands. The cat and the saddle it stands on are ours,
added on 2026-10-09 at the owner's request (see *The cat*).

## Source

- **From:** the designer's BrickLink Studio file, `MOC-34927_yodakya_Xenomorph.io`: 49 parts in
  one step, each posed by hand. No instructions, no building steps.
- **Designer / credit:** yodakya, https://rebrickable.com/mocs/MOC-34927/
- **Saved in `reference/`:** nothing. The Studio file, its unpacked LDraw text
  (`unpacked/model.ldr`) and the designer's picture (`unpacked/thumbnail.png`) are in
  `inbox/xenomorph/`, which is local only: whole instruction files are never committed.
- **Taken in:** 2026-10-08 with `brickkit import`; the joints, the pose and the build order
  finished the same day by xeno-agent.

The torso is a battle droid's (30375), upside down: its shoulder bar is the waist and the
shoulders, its hip bar is the neck, and the stud on its back holds the back.

## The cat

The owner asked for an orange cat on its shoulder or back: BrickLink 13786pb08, the standing
cat with the angry face (Rebrickable 13786pr0024, LDraw 13786p07, element 6401523), one piece.

- **Where it can stand.** A cat stands on studs, and nothing on the designer's back has one
  free: the torso's back stud holds the back's T-bar, and the hinge plate's faces backwards
  into the tubes. Every placing of the cat on those two was tried, at every turn: all collide.
  The only free studs are on the legs.
- **What the back does have** is four free clips, one at the end of each tube. So the cat
  gets a saddle: a 1 x 2 plate with a handle on its end (60478, Black, element 4515368), its
  handle pressed into the end clip of the **outer right tube**, the plate held level behind
  the head. The cat stands on its two studs, looking forward over the right shoulder. That is
  one piece more than the cat; no way was found with none.
- **The two outer tubes droop 10 degrees more** than in the file (2.5 degrees above level in
  the file's attitude, not 12.5), both of them, to keep the pair. At the file's angle the
  saddle's handle sat under 2 LDU from the back end of the head; now the saddle and the cat
  clear everything by 4 LDU and more.
- **How it goes on.** The clip's jaws open up and back, so the handle is pressed in from
  behind and a little above (`insert` on the saddle in `design.py`). Then the cat goes on from
  above. They are the last step.
- **What holds it.** Two friction joints in a row: the tube's clip on the T-bar and the
  tube's end clip on the handle. The cat weighs about a gram and a half, 12 mm out from the
  handle. A clip holds far more than that, but both can be turned: if the saddle droops, level
  it by hand. Not built with real bricks yet.
- **Getting the piece.** The cat was made in Orange with this face for one set (2022), so
  `real_elements` warns that it is rare. It will not be on Pick a Brick; BrickLink has it
  (`out/bricklink_wanted.xml`). Any standing cat (13786) fits the same two studs.

## What changed from the designer's file, and why

Nothing of the designer's was left out: the file's 49 pieces are all here. Two pieces are
added, ours: the cat and its saddle (*The cat*, above). The file's pose has been tipped
back as a whole, and each joint that was only nearly closed has been closed. Distances are in
LDU: 2.5 LDU is a millimetre.

**The pose.** As posed in the file the figure cannot stand. Its middle is 16 LDU behind its
hips and 25 behind its heels, its tail is in the air, and its feet touch only along the front
edges of the toe plates. Left alone it tips back until its tail touches. So:

- **The whole figure is tipped back 7.75 degrees**, the way it tips by itself, until the
  barb's point is on the table. Apart from what is listed here, every joint keeps the angle
  the file gave it.
- **The thighs lean back 31.0 degrees** from upright instead of 38.1: about the same turn
  as the body's. At exactly atan(12/20) the sloped face of the heel
  and the toe's edge lie in one plane, so each foot is flat on the table over 46 LDU. That
  looks like what the feet were made for.
- **The hip piece's stem leans 4.5 degrees forward**, 2 degrees more upright against the
  body than in the file. This is the fine adjustment: it sets the height of everything above
  it, and was found so that the barb's point comes down level with the soles (it is 0.001 LDU
  off).
- **The last tail hinge is at 45 degrees**, two clicks. The file had 36, between two clicks,
  where a locking hinge does not hold.
- **The torso leans 22.5 degrees** in the file's own attitude, not 21.6, so that the hinge at
  the root of the tail is exactly one click (23.4 in the file).
- The figure is moved so that its middle is x = 0, the table is y = 0 and the hip bar is over
  z = 0. In the file the hips and head were on x = 4.75 and the torso and tail on x = 4.5.

**Joints closed.** The checks found the model in 12 pieces, with 7 overlaps. Six of the loose
joints were pieces posed a fraction off their joint's line. The overlaps were the waist pieces
in the tops of the thighs (4), the back T-bar's stem in the wall of the torso's stud (1) and
the jaws of two crest claws in the link under them (2):

**The tail's hinges needed an overlay.** The four links are 30554b, a hinge cylinder with one
click finger at one end and two at the other. LDraw's file for it draws the two-finger end by
including the sub-part `s\480s02.dat` mirrored, and the engine drops a mirrored finger snap
(`brickkit/snaps/shadow.py`, `place()`: SNAP_FGR has no mirror rule). So the engine knew the
part's one-finger end and its hole, but not its two-finger end, and reported the tail in
pieces. That was a gap in the snap data, not in the model, and the guide's cure for one is an
overlay: `brickkit/data/shadow/parts/30554b.dat` adds the missing finger.

No check is switched off and no contact is allowed (`allow_contact`). The one press fit is
the barb, below.

## What had to be worked out, and what is not certain

- **The barb in the axle hole.** The designer pushed the barb's 3.18 mm bar into the
  cross-shaped axle hole of the last link. A round bar is a tight push fit there, held by
  friction on the hole's four inner corners (in LDraw's own shapes they overlap by 0.2 mm). It
  is not a click, and LDCad's snap data has no such joint, so `design.py` declares it a press
  fit (`model.press_fit("barb", ...)`). Fairly sure it holds; it is the one joint here that is
  not a stock connection.
- **The ring's rims as studs.** The foot plate and the heel sit on the two rims of the ring of
  a 4081b, which LDCad lists as studs. They are the diameter of a stud and hollow.
- **Flat feet.** The file has the thighs 7 degrees further back, which stands the figure on
  the front edges of its toe plates. Laying the heels' slopes flat is our reading of what the
  feet are for.
- **It leans on its tail.** Two feet and a barb's point are all that touch the table. The
  check passes, but the clips at the hips and the waist have to hold the body's weight by
  friction, as on any figure like this. If the barb lifts, tip the body back at the hips.
- **The claws go 17 LDU into the arms.** LDCad gives the arm's hole as 10 deep; LDraw's shape
  of 98313 has it 19 deep, so the file's depth was kept.
- **The build order** is ours. It was arranged so that every joint has a straight way on:
  legs slide along the hip bar, the neck goes on before the head (the built head cannot be
  pushed straight onto the torso's bar: its back plate is in the way), the back's clip plate
  goes on its T-bar before the T-bar goes into the torso, and the sting is made before it is
  clicked on.
- **The lean is inside a narrow window for the video.** The Quick Bricks planner pushes a
  hinge on along one of eight directions 45 degrees apart, and a two-finger end only comes off
  a finger cleanly within a few degrees of the finger's own line. With the body tipped back
  between 7.5 and 8.2 degrees every hinge of the tail has a clear way; at 7.45 or 8.5 the
  sting does not. 7.75 is the middle. Change `LEAN` and `HIPS` together (the comment in
  `design.py` says how) and run `brickkit status xenomorph --check` again.
- **Its size** is measured from the model: 271 x 168 x 56 LDU.
