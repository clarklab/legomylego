# Darth Vader PEZ

A Quick Bricks model: 53 pieces, 10.6 cm tall, about the size of a real PEZ dispenser. It is a
black Darth Vader helmet (two round eyes, a grey nose, a silver mouth grille, a flare each
side) on a black stem with a small foot, and the head tips back on a click hinge to show a pink
sweet, as a real one does. The head is 3.7 cm wide over the flares and the foot 1.6 cm by
3.2 cm. It is our own design, made from three pictures of a real dispenser, and one of three
PEZ models on the same stem (`brickkit/designs/pez.py`; the others are `pez_stormtrooper` and
`pez_yoda`). All 8 checks pass; the stability check's limit is lowered from 10 degrees to 6
(see below).

## Source

- **From:** three pictures of a real Darth Vader PEZ dispenser, given by the owner in
  `inbox/pez-darth-vader/`. They show the real thing, not a brick model, so there are no
  building steps to copy. The owner asked for the three "as close to real life size as
  possible", each on "the same base, in the correct color", with faces "as close as you can
  get" and heads that "hinge like a real pez".
- **Designer / credit:** our own design, by lead (Claude) to the owner's brief. No brick model
  was copied, so no credit line is needed. Who took the pictures is not known.
- **Saved in `reference/`:** `picture_01.jpeg`: a real one on a purple cloth, from the front
  and a little to one side; it shows the wide flare of the helmet, the silver mouth, the raised
  letters on the stem and the thin foot. `picture_02.jpeg`: a real one from the side on white;
  it shows the helmet in profile and how far back the flare goes. `picture_03.webp`: the
  dispenser from the front, with a Star Wars logo; it shows the dome with a ridge down the
  middle and the striped triangle of the mouth grille. All are local only: git ignores
  `models/pez_*/reference/`. The originals stay in the inbox.
- **Taken in:** 2026-10-10 by lead (Claude).

## What had to be worked out

Everything: the pictures show a moulded plastic toy, and the bricks are ours. These are the
choices, and what is not like the real thing.

- **The size.** A real dispenser is about 10.5 cm tall with its head. That is the figure the
  design was made to; the pictures carry no scale and no dispenser was measured. Ours is 264
  LDU: 10.6 cm. The foot is a 2 x 4 plate, 1.6 cm by 3.2 cm, long from front to back. The stem
  is one stud wide and two deep and, with its tiles, 14.4 mm by 16 mm and 6.4 cm tall. The
  head is three studs square at its core, 2.9 cm tall and 3.7 cm wide over the flares.
- **The stem.** Four rounds of two 1 x 2 plates and two bricks with a stud on each side
  (47905), and two 2 x 4 tiles standing on those studs each side for the smooth sides. Two
  jumper plates centre the one-stud stem on the two-stud foot. It is solid: there is no
  magazine and no spring.
- **The sweet.** One 1 x 2 tile on top of the stem, which does not come out. The head rests
  on it when shut, and it shows when the head is tipped back. Its colour (Dark Pink) is our
  choice; the pictures show no sweets.
- **The hinge.** Two locking hinge plates: the one with two fingers (54657) flat on top of the
  stem, its fingers past the back, and the one with one finger (44301b) upright on the back of
  the head, finger down. They click every 22.5 degrees, and the design tips the head back
  three clicks: 67.5 degrees. The mechanism check sweeps 24 poses of it and finds nothing in
  the way. The head is held by the hinge alone; the sweet under it is a tile and grips
  nothing.
- **Which hinge plates.** The older moulds of the same pair are dated: in Black, 44301a was
  last in a set in 2014 and 44302a in 2020. 44301b and 54657 are in sets up to 2026 (136 and
  111 sets in Black).
- **The head's core.** Eight tall bricks with two studs on one side (32952) in a ring, hollow
  in the middle, tied by the 3 x 3 plate on top. Three face the front and carry the mask, two
  face out on each side, and one faces back for the hinge. Each side's two bricks are joined
  first by what goes on their studs (a cheek), so nothing stands loose while the head is
  built.
- **What in the face stands for what.** The two black round tiles are the eyes. The dark grey
  cheese slope between them, high edge up, is the nose of the mask. The Flat Silver plate
  with a tooth, tooth down, is the mouth grille: the triangle of the real one. The black
  curved slope on each side, thick end down, is the flare of the helmet. The two 3 x 3
  plates are its brim, and the four curved slopes its dome.
- **The mouth hangs below the head.** The tooth reaches 8 mm below the head's underside, in
  front of the top of the stem, and from straight ahead it hides most of the sweet's front
  edge when the head is shut (`out/renders/front.png`). It swings up with the head; the
  mechanism check finds nothing in its way. The piece is a plate, so its one stud faces
  forward in the middle of the mouth.
- **What could not be done at this size.** In the pictures the flare runs all round the sides
  and the back of the helmet and comes down past the face. Here it is one curved slope each
  side, 6.4 mm out, and nothing at the back, where the hinge is. The real helmet's brow
  overhangs the mask; here the brim is no wider than the head, and the face stands two plates
  in front of it. The eyes are black tiles on black bricks, so they show by their shape only.
  Of the dark grey, only the nose and the edges of the plate behind the mouth show. The ridge
  down the middle of the dome is left out. So are the stem's raised letters, and the foot is
  two plates thick where the real one is thin.
- **The curved slopes' cut-outs.** A curved slope (11477, and the 2 x 2 one, 15068) has a
  cut-out one plate high under its thick end. So each of the dome's four holds by the one
  stud under its thin end, and the stud under its thick end shows in the cut-out: a small
  notch in the middle of each side of the dome. Each flare holds by the two upper studs of its
  cheek, one on each brick, which is what joins the pair; the two lower studs sit in its
  cut-out, and the front one shows from the front (`out/renders/close_head.png`). The step's
  caption in `design.py` says the slope goes "on their four studs"; it grips two of them.
- **Does it stand?** Like the real thing, only just: it is tall on a small foot. It weighs
  about 30 g and tips to the side at 7.4 degrees, the foot being two studs wide (the check
  wants at least 10). So `[checks.stability] min_tilt_deg = 6` in `model.toml`, with a
  comment.
- **Not tried in bricks.** The checks are on the computer model: 91 connections (90 stud, 1
  hinge) in one assembly, no overlaps, every piece with a clear way on. No physical build is
  recorded, so whether the hinge holds the head up when it is tipped back is not known.

## Parts and price

- 53 pieces in 19 lines. Every line has a LEGO element ID; none is flagged rare.
  `out/parts.csv`, `out/pick_a_brick.csv` and `out/bricklink_wanted.xml` are ready to upload.

## Quick Bricks

- `[quick]` in `model.toml`: the red LEGO floor (`lego_red`: black on Imperial red) in the
  night room, daylight (in the warm evening light the black came out brown), 18 seconds,
  clicks only.
- `highlight = ["eyes", "mouth", "sweet"]`, three close-ups: the sweet as it goes on the stem,
  then the mouth and an eye as the head is built.
- `flex = 2.8`: in the last seconds before the cut the head tips back on its hinge (the
  mechanism's own pose, eased), holds while the sweet shows, and shuts again, so the video
  ends on the face.
- With 53 pieces the parts come in from off frame (the opening with the parts laid out is for
  models under 25).
- The video's build plan is clean, audited the way `brickkit status pez_darth_vader --check`
  does it: no piece passes through another and each lands on something.
- `out/renders_open/three_quarter.png` is a still with the head tipped back and the sweet
  showing.
- The final video was not rendered when this was written; `brickkit status` shows where it is.
  `collection = "quick_bricks"` is not set: it is not on the site.
